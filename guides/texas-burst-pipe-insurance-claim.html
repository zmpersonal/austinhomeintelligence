Insurance & water damage

Texas Burst-Pipe Insurance Claim Guide

A practical Texas homeowner workflow for sudden pipe failures: stop the water, document damage, mitigate further loss, and understand the difference between sudden and gradual damage.

Updated August 21, 2026Austin / TexasPrimary-source linked
Direct answer

Sudden and accidental water damage is commonly treated differently from gradual leakage.

Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage such as a burst pipe, toilet overflow or broken washer hose, while gradual leaks or seepage are usually not covered.

First actions after a burst pipe

  1. Shut off the water source as quickly as it is safe to do so.
  2. Protect belongings and move wet items where practical.
  3. Photograph or video the damage before permanent repairs.
  4. Begin reasonable drying/mitigation to prevent additional damage.
  5. Notify the insurer promptly and keep receipts.

What the cause changes

Insurance outcomes are policy-specific, but the distinction between a sudden event and a long-running leak matters. Flood water is also treated separately from a standard homeowners policy.

If mold appears later

TDI notes that mold that develops on property damaged by a covered sudden water event may be treated differently from mold caused by gradual seepage. Separate mold cleanup/testing coverage can also vary by policy.

Build a clean claim file

Keep the loss date, photos, videos, damaged-item list, temporary-repair receipts, mitigation invoices and contractor estimates together. Avoid throwing away damaged property before the adjuster has had an opportunity to inspect unless safety requires it.
