Water damage

Austin Water Damage: What to Do in the First 24 Hours

A practical Austin homeowner response guide for leaks, burst pipes and indoor water damage, including safety, documentation, drying and when restoration or insurance involvement becomes reasonable.

Updated August 21, 2026Austin / TexasPrimary-source linked
Direct answer

Stop the source, document the loss, and start safe mitigation quickly.

Texas insurance guidance emphasizes stopping the water, protecting property, documenting damage and beginning drying/mitigation. The correct contractor path depends on the source, contamination level, damage extent and whether regulated mold work becomes involved.

First 30 minutes

  1. Stop the water if it can be done safely.
  2. Turn off power to affected areas if electrical hazards exist and you can do so safely.
  3. Move valuables away from active water.
  4. Photograph the source and affected rooms before major cleanup.

First several hours

Remove standing water where safe, increase drying, separate wet materials and document what is being discarded. If the affected area is large, contaminated, hidden in walls/ceilings or impossible to dry safely, professional mitigation becomes more reasonable.

Insurance documentation

Report covered losses promptly, preserve photos and receipts, and avoid unnecessary permanent repairs before the carrier has had a reasonable opportunity to inspect. Temporary protective work is different from rebuilding.

Moisture source first

Mold risk rises when materials stay wet. Texas insurance guidance says mold can start growing within a day after a leak, but the actual outcome depends on material, moisture, temperature and drying. The practical priority is fast moisture control rather than a universal countdown.
