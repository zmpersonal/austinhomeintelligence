Trees & permits

Austin Protected Tree Permit Guide

How Austin regulates protected and heritage trees, how to measure trunk diameter, and when a residential project can trigger Tree Ordinance Review.

Updated August 21, 2026Austin / TexasPrimary-source linked
Direct answer

A 19-inch tree can change the project.

On one- and two-unit residential property in Austin, trees 19 inches or larger in diameter are regulated. Removal or certain impacts can require Tree Ordinance Review. Heritage protections can apply at 24 inches for listed species.

The quick rule

Protected tree19+ inches DBH

All species are protected at this size on residential property.

Heritage tree24+ inches DBH

Applies to listed species, including all oaks and pecans.

Austin measures diameter at about 4.5 feet above grade. A regulated-tree issue is not limited to full removal: the City says pruning 25% or more of canopy or disturbing the critical root zone can also trigger review.

How to measure before calling anyone

Measure the trunk circumference 4.5 feet above ground and divide by 3.14 to estimate diameter. Multi-stemmed or irregular trees use special measurement rules, so use the City Arborist instructions when the result is near a threshold.

Do not let a quote outrun the permit check.

If a contractor proposes removal of a large tree, confirm whether a TORA is required before authorizing work.

What if the tree is dead or dangerous?

A dead, diseased, or imminent-hazard condition does not automatically erase regulation. The City publishes a specific review process for regulated trees that are dead, diseased, or hazardous.

What to ask a tree company

  • Will you identify whether the tree is protected or heritage?
  • Who handles any TORA submission?
  • Is an ISA Certified Arborist involved in the assessment?
  • Does the written scope distinguish trimming from removal and include haul-away?
