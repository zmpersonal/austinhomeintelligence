Before you approve work

Do I need a permit to replace my AC in Austin?

A quick way to think it through before a contractor brings it up.

1

Replacing the whole system?

A full HVAC replacement in Austin generally requires a mechanical permit pulled by a licensed contractor.

2

Just a like-for-like repair?

Many straightforward repairs (capacitor, motor, minor component swaps) typically don't require a separate permit — but rules and scope interpretations vary.

3

Not sure which one this is?

Ask the contractor directly whether they are pulling a permit, and confirm they're licensed to do so in Austin before work starts.

FAQ

Questions Austin homeowners actually ask about AC.

How much does AC replacement cost in Austin?

Total cost depends heavily on tonnage, equipment efficiency (SEER2), ductwork condition, electrical work and whether a permit is required — which is why wide national averages are hard to trust for a specific house. We're building a scope-normalized Austin pricing dataset instead of publishing a single number today; see the cost & provider intelligence section above.

Is $15,000 too much for HVAC replacement?

It depends on what's actually included — system size and efficiency, ductwork changes, electrical upgrades, permits, thermostat and warranty terms — not just the final number. Ask your contractor to itemize each of those before comparing quotes.

Should I repair or replace a 14-year-old AC?

A commonly used industry guideline multiplies the system's age by the repair cost; a high result relative to full replacement cost tends to favor replacement. Repair history, efficiency and comfort complaints matter too. Our free AC Risk Check walks through this using your own numbers.

Do I need a permit to replace my AC in Austin?

A full system replacement generally requires a mechanical permit pulled by a licensed contractor. Straightforward like-for-like repairs typically don't require a separate permit, though scope and interpretation can vary. See our Austin HVAC Permit Guide for details.

How do I choose an HVAC company in Austin?

Reviews and star ratings alone don't confirm sizing, permits, scope or warranty. Check licensing, insurance, service area, permit competency, quote completeness, emergency availability and warranty terms — see our provider-selection criteria above.

What should an AC repair cost?

It depends on the failed component, whether refrigerant is involved, and labor time — a capacitor and a compressor are not comparable repairs. We're building scope-normalized Austin repair pricing rather than publishing an unverified range today.

When should I service my AC?

Most manufacturers recommend at least one professional service visit per year, ideally scheduled before peak Austin cooling season rather than during it.

Why is my upstairs hotter than the rest of the house?

Common causes include duct balancing issues, insulation differences, and undersized returns upstairs. It's worth describing to a technician specifically rather than assuming it's simply that heat rises.

Why did my electric bill jump?

Possible causes include seasonal heat, an aging or less efficient system, thermostat habits, or a developing mechanical issue. Our AC Risk Check helps narrow down which of those is most likely at your address.

Is duct cleaning actually necessary?

Not automatically. It depends on visible evidence such as debris, suspected mold or pest contamination — not a routine calendar. See our Duct Cleaning Check and duct cleaning guide for when cleaning is actually justified.

Methodology

Facts where we have them. Caution where we don't.

A risk score should help you make a decision on your AC — not make it sound scarier than it is.

01

Public facts

NWS forecasts, City of Austin permit records and published mechanical/permit rules.

02

Your information

System age, symptoms, repair history and maintenance habits you tell us about.

03

Estimates

Potential costs or modeled values, disclosed as estimates rather than facts.

04

Risk signals

Rules and heuristics that help put AC issues in priority order — not a prediction of failure.

A high score does not mean your AC will fail. A low score does not guarantee it's fine. When the evidence doesn't support urgent action, the right recommendation may be to watch it, maintain it, or do nothing for now.
